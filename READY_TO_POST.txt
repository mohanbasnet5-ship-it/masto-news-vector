PM Shah Commits to Resolving India Border Dispute Through Dialogue

Prime Minister Balendra Shah told Parliament on Sunday that Nepal will settle its long-disputed border with India through negotiation and diplomacy, not unilateral claims. The commitment matters because Kathmandu and Delhi have clashed over three territories—Lipulekh, Limpiyadhura, and Kalapani—since Nepal's 2020 map revision, with India rejecting those claims as historically baseless. This matters because ordinary Nepalis in border districts like Darchula face uncertainty over land rights, trade routes, and which nation controls access to pilgrimage sites like Kailash Manasarovar, while families in Kathmandu worry how prolonged tension could affect jobs and cross-border commerce.

प्रधानमन्त्री बलेन्द्र शाहले आइतवार संसदमा नेपालले भारतसँगको विवादास्पद सीमा समस्या एकतर्फी दावीको सट्टा वार्ता र कूटनीतिमार्फत समाधान गर्नेछ भन्ने प्रतिबद्धता जनाएका छन्। नेपालले २०२० मा संशोधित गरेको नक्साअनुसार लिपुलेख, लिम्पियाधुरा र कालापानी गरी तीन क्षेत्रको दावी गरेपछि भारतले ती दावीलाई ऐतिहासिक रूपमा आधारहीन भनी अस्वीकार गरेको छ। दार्चुला जस्ता सीमाबर्तী जिल्लामा बस्ने आम नेपालीले जमिनको अधिकार, व्यापार मार्ग र कैलाश मानसरोवर जस्ता तीर्थस्थलमा कुन देशको नियन्त्रण हुनेछ भन्नेबारेमा अनिश्चितता सहन गर्दैछन्।

Nepal taps private partners to build 400 kV West Seti power line

Nepal will construct the 400 kV West Seti transmission line using a public-private partnership model instead of direct government investment, shifting infrastructure risk to private developers. PPP projects in Nepal's energy sector have faced delays and cost overruns, most recently with the Kathmandu-Hetauda 400 kV line. Nepal PM Reignites Border Fight: Kalapani, Lipulekh Dispute Back on Table

Prime Minister Balendra Shah told Parliament that Nepal has formally notified India of territorial encroachments in Lipulekh, Kalapani, and Limpiyadhura—a decades-old dispute rooted in an 1816 treaty that never clearly defined where the Kali River begins. Nepal argues the river's source sits at Limpiyadhura, placing the disputed Himalayan region inside Nepal; India claims it starts near Kalapani, on their side. Both nations agreed to resolve this through historians and diplomats, but Shah's public statement forced Nepal's foreign ministry to clarify it was not a formal territorial claim. This matters because if Nepal succeeds in this diplomatic push, control of this strategically vital tri-border zone near China could shift, affecting grazing rights, military positioning, and resource access for herders and border communities who live in the shadow of this dispute.

Both Nations Encroaching on Each Other, PM Shah Tells Parliament

Nepal PM Balendra Shah acknowledged Sunday that India and Nepal have both crossed into each other's territory, breaking new ground in public statements on the border dispute. Shah told Parliament that both governments agreed to form teams of historians, surveyors and experts to resolve the decades-old conflict through talks, and that he has already discussed the matter with India, China and the UK. The dispute has simmered since May when Nepal objected to India resuming the Kailash Mansarovar pilgrimage through the Lipulekh Pass that Kathmandu claims.
